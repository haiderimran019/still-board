The idea

Three places. Not twenty.

Most task apps let you make infinite lists, which is how a to-do list turns into a second source of guilt. This one has three sections and refuses to grow.

Now

What you're carrying today. Seven at most — the app will stop you.

Next

Soon, but not today. No count, no pressure, no red badges.

Someday

Things that can wait. They fade over weeks and fold into a box.

7

The limit is the feature

Try to put an eighth thing in Now and the board turns around and shows you the seven already there. Finish one, move one, or drop one. It won't quietly file it somewhere else for you — that decision is the entire point of the app.

What's in it

Small, and finished.

Yours alone

Every note lives on your phone, in a local database. No account, no server, nothing uploaded.

Works with no signal

There's nothing to load. Open it on the bus, in a basement, on campus wifi that isn't.

Drag it around

Hold a note and move it between sections, or swipe it to edit or clear.

Colour by urgency

Mark what's burning. Red, amber, green — or nothing at all, which is most things.

Quiet reminders

Only on Now, only three choices, no date picker. Local notifications, nothing sent anywhere.

Keep a copy

Save the whole board to a file, or back it up to a private folder in your own Google Drive.

Getting it

Not in the stores yet.

It's being built in the open, starting with students at one university in Pakistan. The code is on GitHub, and you can run it on your own phone today with Expo.
